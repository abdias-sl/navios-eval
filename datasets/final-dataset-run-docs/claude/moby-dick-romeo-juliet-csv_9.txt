"Why does the author use a ""Bibliographical system"" to classify whales?","The author deploys a ""Bibliographical system"" to classify whales because he finds traditional taxonomic methods inadequate for these creatures. He explains that the usual anatomical distinctions among whales are not helpful for classification, stating that ""if you descend into the bowels of various leviathans, the distinctions are not a fiftieth part as available to a systematizer as external ones.""

He points out that attempts to classify whales based on external features like baleen, hump, fin, or teeth have failed because these characteristics appear inconsistently across different whale species, forming ""irregular combinations"" that ""utterly defy all general methodization.""

After concluding that all previous whale-naturalists have ""split"" on this ""rock"" of classification, he declares: ""What then remains? nothing but to take hold of the whales bodily, in their entire liberal volume, and boldly sort them that way."" Thus, the Bibliographical system based on size (Folio, Octavo, and Duodecimo) is presented as ""the only one that can possibly succeed, for it alone is practicable."""
